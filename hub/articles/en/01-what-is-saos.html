What Is SAOS, and Why Should You Care

Reading time: 3 minutes · technical background required: none

Start with a story

Imagine you run a small business. You keep a notebook. Everything that happens, money in, money out, who paid, who promised what, goes in the notebook.

One day someone claims they paid you. You open the notebook, nothing there. They say: "I swear I paid!" You say: "I swear I never received it!"

Who's right? Nobody knows. This is the oldest problem on the internet: everyone makes claims, and no one can prove anything.

So we invented witnesses

Humanity's classic fix: witnesses. A notary. An accountant. A court. Someone trusted stamps a document: "I saw this happen, on this date."

The problem: witnesses cost money, witnesses are slow, and witnesses, how to put this gently, can be compromised.

The SAOS idea, in one sentence

SAOS photographs your notebook every 36 seconds and files the photograph with five independent public registries in different parts of the world, for less than a cent.

Let's unpack that:

  • The notebook is the system's journal. Every action gets recorded.
  • The photograph is a mathematical fingerprint of the entire notebook, unique, like a real fingerprint. Change a single comma and the fingerprint changes completely.
  • The five registries are public blockchains: Ethereum, Solana, Steem, Hive, TRON. Think of them as giant public record offices that nobody can erase, run by thousands of independent strangers who don't coordinate with each other or with us.
  • Less than a cent, some registries are completely free (Steem and Hive run on stake-based resource credits), and the rest cost fractions of a cent, measured on real transactions.

Why can't anyone cheat if there are photographs?

That's exactly the beauty of it. Once the fingerprint is filed with five registries, you cannot alter the notebook without getting caught. To fake history, an attacker would have to break into five independent systems at once, each guarded by thousands of computers worldwide. That's not hard. That's impossible.

And if someone tries anyway? The fingerprints won't match. Everyone sees it. Immediately.

"Okay: what do I get from this?"

Depends who you are:

If you're a regular user, when someone promises "your money is safe," "the transaction went through," "the product exists," you no longer have to believe them. You can check it yourself, with a link, for free, in a minute.

If you run a business, every promise to a customer becomes an unerasable receipt. You said "we shipped at 14:32"? There's a stamp. No competitor can claim your customer "misremembers."

If you work with AI agents, today every agent can claim "task completed." With SAOS there's a receipt. Our whole thesis fits in one line: every agent can claim anything; only a public chain cannot lie.

If you're a developer, there's a zero-dependency SDK, live in the repo and verified end to end: one API call proves whether a legacy Steem/Hive account and an EVM address belong to the same key. saos-id-verify (npm publish in progress). Runs anywhere JavaScript runs.

If you have an old Steem or Hive account, your identity can finally work in the new world too, without ever exposing your keys (article 05 covers this).

And the cost?

Here's the real, unrounded number: one permanent notarized record costs eight millionths of a dollar in a 10,000-claim batch. Less than a grain of sand. And it's not a promotional price, it's measured against real mainnet transactions, exact to the wei.

The bottom line

SAOS isn't "another coin." It's a truth layer, the place where claims become facts. Instead of "trust me," you get "verify me."

And we mean that literally: everything written here is backed by links to real records anyone can open. Don't trust us, check. That's the whole point.
