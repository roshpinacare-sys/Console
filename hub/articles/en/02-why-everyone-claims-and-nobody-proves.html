Why the Internet Lies to You, and How to Fix It Once and for All

Reading time: 4 minutes · technical background required: none

The problem nobody talks about

Open any website, any app, any chat with a bot. What do you see? Claims.

"Your order has shipped." "Your money is protected." "Task completed." "A million satisfied customers." "Your data is private."

All of them are claims. And how do you know whether they're true? You don't. You believe them. And that's exactly the point: the entire internet is built on blind faith.

It used to be less bad

In the old world, when you wanted to verify something, you had a notary, an accountant, a bank, a court. Institutions. People with a stamp whose job was to say "I confirm this happened."

Then the internet arrived, and suddenly: no stamp, no institution, no witnesses. There's a screen, and behind it someone who can write whatever they like.

The attempts to fix it, and why they failed

Certificates and attestations? Expensive, slow, and you have to trust whoever issued them.

Giant companies that "protect your data"? They get breached themselves. Every few months there's a new headline: hundreds of millions of records leaked. Who guards the guards?

And the solution that sounds most promising, blockchain? It's true that a blockchain is a public ledger nothing can be erased from. But in practice it's complicated, expensive (every record costs real money), and most people, including most developers, give up on it.

What we did at SAOS: took the best of every world

We asked: what if you could have both a ledger that can't be erased, and a price close to nothing, and the simplicity of sending a message?

So we built a system like that. It works like this:

Step 1, the notebook. Everything that happens in the system gets written down, in order, line after line. No gaps, no erasures.

Step 2, the stamp. Every 36 seconds the system "photographs" the entire notebook and produces a unique fingerprint from it, a string of characters that can't be forged. Change one comma, and the fingerprint changes completely.

Step 3, the witnesses. The fingerprint is sent simultaneously to several giant, independent public ledgers. Some of them are completely free. Some cost fractions of an agora. None of them belong to us, and none of them can be bribed, because they're run by thousands of different people around the world who don't know each other.

Step 4, the check. Now anyone, you, your competitors, an accountant, a court, can open the public ledger and compare. Fingerprints match? Everything is true. They don't match? Someone lied, and everyone can see it.

"Wait, doesn't this cost a fortune?"

That's the best part. Here's a real price, measured in practice: one permanent record costs less than a hundred agorot, that is, less than a single shekel. And we're not quoting a theoretical price list. This is the price of real transactions already recorded today in the public ledgers, with a receipt anyone can open and check.

For comparison: a human notary charges hundreds of shekels for a single document. We record ten thousand records for less than a dollar.

And what about your own mistakes?

Here comes the part we're proudest of. A few weeks ago we discovered we'd been wrong: we had reported that one of the networks was "blocked" for us, when in fact it was a bug in our own reading of the data. We had 52 times more resources than we thought.

What did we do? We published it. We wrote about it, fixed it, and built checks that make sure this particular mistake can't happen again.

Why does that matter to you? Because a system capable of saying "we were wrong" in public is a system you can trust on everything else. A system that hides small mistakes will hide big ones too.

The bottom line

The internet suffers from one disease: everyone claims, nobody proves. SAOS is the simple cure: every claim gets a receipt. Every receipt is open to the public. And nobody can erase any receipt, ever.

It's not magic. It's orderly record keeping, independent witnesses, and math that works.
